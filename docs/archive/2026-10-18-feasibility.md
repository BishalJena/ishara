# Preserve ordinary calls while translating signs

> Historical feasibility synthesis. Its optional-Bolna and hard no-pivot assumptions
> are superseded. Use the [handoff](2026-10-28-handoff.md) and [active plan](../../PLAN.md)
> for current scope, capability and next steps.

**The best current candidate preserves the user's ordinary cellular Phone call from their own number, adds foreground Ishara sign capture, and injects approved speech through Apple's public AAC mechanism.** System Live Captions is a candidate return channel for the user; it does not give Ishara an incoming transcript. This fits the user's clarified goal more closely than a service-originated call, callback, or two-call bridge, all of which are excluded. The target device is an iPhone 17, but its installed iOS version is unknown and **camera + AAC injection + system captions have not been tested together**. A small, bounded ISL conversation is a credible research objective, not an established capability: recognition, utterance boundaries, unknown rejection, and natural phrase coverage remain unvalidated. Bolna authentication and voice-catalog access passed read-only checks, but no documented standalone Bolna text-to-audio endpoint was established in the pages inspected. Research completed on **18 October 2026** supports the device and data experiments below; it does not authorize implementation or establish a ready-to-build architecture.

## Native speech preserves the call, but captions stay with the user

Apple documents synthesized app speech entering an already-active Phone, FaceTime, or compatible voice-processing VoIP call from iOS 18.2 onward. The mechanism requires the system **Add Audio in Calls** setting, app permission, a usage description, and microphone-injection availability handling. Apple also documents opening another app while a Phone call continues. Together these support investigating a normal call followed by foreground Ishara; neither source certifies concurrent camera inference and captions. ([Apple AAC sample](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/adding-synthesized-speech-to-calls), [ongoing Phone calls](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/while-on-a-call-iph3c9951d7/ios))

The proposed minimum workflow is one-time accessibility/camera onboarding, ordinary calling through Phone or Contacts, an explicit transition into Ishara, hands-free signing with readable system captions, and native Phone controls to hang up. Camera-only capture is an engineering strategy to avoid competing for microphone input, not a proven session configuration. Ordinary carrier identity is preserved by keeping the existing call; actual caller-ID display remains carrier-dependent. **Stopping Ishara capture does not establish authority to end the unrelated Phone call.** Call observation is distinct from call control. ([Call observer](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/callkit/cxcallobserver), [cellular conversation manager](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/livecommunicationkit/telephonyconversationmanager))

Apple's developer support states that no supported API gives an unrelated app live audio from a call it does not carry. CallKit supplies coordination and calling UI, rather than access to another app's media. Therefore Ishara cannot assume it hears the receptionist, reads the system caption overlay, simplifies the incoming transcript, detects the offered time, or automatically knows that a booking succeeded. The human reads the system captions and deliberately responds. A model sees only supported signing observations, approved user information, explicitly selected scenario, and Ishara's own history. ([Apple DTS explanation](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/841340))

This boundary changes the earlier contextual-decision example. If the receptionist offers four o'clock, Ishara cannot expand a nod into “Yes, four o'clock works” merely because that answer is conversationally likely. It has not observed four o'clock. An approved literal affirmative or an explicitly communicated time could be spoken; automatically accepting an unseen offer would fabricate consent. The same limit prevents silently inferring a yes/no response state from the receptionist's question. Deliberate nod/shake gating remains an interaction and recognition experiment.

Audio behavior also constrains the interface. Apple says injection mixes spoken app audio alongside microphone audio, and **native call mute also mutes the injected speech**. The consulted `spokenAudio` and setter documentation does not promise room-microphone ducking or suppression. A quiet demo, external audio route, or other noise handling must be evaluated rather than assumed. ([Injection mode](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudiosession/microphoneinjectionmode), [spokenAudio](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudiosession/microphoneinjectionmode/spokenaudio))

Dynamic Island is a secondary status/return surface, not an autonomous capture engine. Conditional camera multitasking/PiP mechanisms exist, so blanket claims that all background camera use is impossible are outdated; those mechanisms do not establish eligibility for this non-VoIP accessibility sidecar. Apple advises against enabling VoIP background mode for a non-VoIP app. Foreground capture is therefore the strongest initial hypothesis, with app switches, screen lock and camera interruptions made visible. A default cellular dialer is a separate, region-restricted path: Apple's guide requires an EU-registered developer account and EU-located device for testing. It is not the general India demo solution. ([ActivityKit](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/displaying-live-data-with-live-activities), [camera multitasking](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avcapturesession/ismultitaskingcameraaccesssupported), [Apple DTS on VoIP mode](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/791671), [default dialer guide](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/livecommunicationkit/preparing-your-app-to-be-the-default-dialer-app))

## The seamless return channel depends on actual language support

Apple's Live Captions guide describes captions of iPhone audio, a movable window, and support for iPhone 11 and newer. That makes an overlay above foreground Ishara a concrete experiment, not a demonstrated composition. The latest feature-availability page consulted explicitly targets **iOS/iPadOS 27** and lists **English (India)** for Live Captions, but not Hindi. An iPhone 17 hardware specification does not tell us which OS, caption locales, assets or regional settings are installed. An update should be considered only after inspecting those facts and the exact feature requirement. ([Live Captions guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/get-live-captions-of-spoken-audio-iphe0990f7bb/ios), [current feature availability](https://www.apple.com/in/ios/feature-availability/#accessibility-live-captions))

**English (India) transcription is not Hindi-to-English translation.** The current Phone Live Translation guide does not list Hindi, and describes translation in the Phone interface without establishing that translated captions float over Ishara's foreground camera. The Translate app's language list cannot establish a Phone feature. If Hindi or mixed-language receptionist speech is essential, the native return channel remains unresolved; adding an ASR SDK cannot supply audio the app cannot access. ([Phone-specific Live Translation guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/see-translations-during-phone-calls-ly3pl0v38sek/ios), [Apple Intelligence conditions](https://support.apple.com/en-us/121115))

The working persona is an adult ISL signer with a smartphone, not a validated representative of all Deaf users. The preferred readable language, literacy, caption speed tolerance, sign variant, correction preferences and camera posture are unknown. W3C emphasizes hands, arms, face and body as part of signing and cautions that written captions do not suit everyone equally. A propped phone that exposes both hands and face while leaving captions legible is a physical hypothesis to test. Online guidance and held-out dataset evaluation can reduce unsupported assumptions, but hearing team members reenacting signs cannot validate Deaf usability or natural ISL. ([W3C signing guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/media/av/sign-languages/), [Deaf Connect framing guide](https://deafconnect.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/221206_Deaf-Connect-VRI-Instructional-Guide.pdf))

Existing Indian human relay approaches should inform this comparison. ISLRTC announced VRS integration into helpline 14456, and SignAble describes ISL VRI/VRS. These sources establish service claims, not an assessment of current availability or a demonstration that automated recognition replaces professional interpretation. Ishara's defensible near-term claim is a bounded sign-mediated conversation experiment. ([Government announcement](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2098822), [SignAble](https://signable.live/))

## Bounded natural phrases are a better target than word counts

The inherited **62% top-1 / 83% top-3 on 26 words** remains unverified because no underlying artifact, split, script or confusion matrix was reviewed. It is not live-phone accuracy, and top-three recall is not automatic speech accuracy. The reconciled historical inventory of 25 signs and two proposed head gestures is now recorded in the [recognition note](2026-10-18-recognition-notes.md#3-what-vocabulary-interaction-policy-and-comparison-plan-are-defensible). It includes low-utility appointment concepts while omitting important repair concepts; neither its usefulness nor exact dataset coverage is established. It should follow dialogue coverage and source-clip auditing rather than become a requirement by repetition.

Isolated data and phrase/sentence data both exist. INCLUDE contains 263 isolated classes, with the original paper describing 4,287 videos and the archive explaining five later additions to reach 4,292. CISLR covers roughly 4,700 words. iSign reports 118,228 video–text pairs; ISLTranslate supplies a continuous phrase/sentence resource. These resources justify an audit, not a claim that the required appointment dialogue is covered or that a pretrained iPhone translator is available. Dataset terms must be separated from repository code licenses: INCLUDE specifies CC-BY-4.0; CISLR's dataset section specifies non-commercial terms; iSign's public README specifies CC-BY-NC-SA-4.0 plus research/non-commercial/no-further-redistribution conditions, whose compatibility and application require qualification before asset reuse; gated video access was not obtained. ISLRTC dictionary permission includes acknowledgment and no resale/profiteering, rather than blanket commercial reuse. ([INCLUDE archive](https://zenodo.org/records/4010759), [INCLUDE paper](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3394171.3413528), [CISLR](https://github.com/Exploration-Lab/CISLR), [iSign README](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Exploration-Lab/iSign/blob/main/README.md), [ISLTranslate](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-acl.665/), [ISLRTC FAQ](https://islrtc.nic.in/faq/))

A compact temporal landmark recognizer is the leading baseline to investigate because movement matters, while a hand detector supplies geometry rather than language. An isolated temporal encoder still does not solve continuous boundaries, transitions, unknown signing, or ISL grammar. A 2026 specialist paper illustrates the generalization risk: 95.88% on the original INCLUDE split falls to 83.82% on its approximate pseudo-signer protocol and 19.20% on CISLR. Those author-reported results were not reproduced and do not measure this device or Jev. A bounded whole-phrase recognizer or retrieval approach deserves comparison if suitable naturally signed phrase examples and terms exist. Requiring English gloss order or hidden tap-to-delimit steps would compromise the stated seamless objective and must be disclosed as a fallback choice. ([Specialist study](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026W/MSLR/papers/Varanasi_Isolated_Sign_Language_Recognition_via_MediaPipe_Landmarks_A_Case_Study_CVPRW_2026_paper.pdf), [MediaPipe iOS guide](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/hand_landmarker/ios))

Jev is a legitimate **experimental visual comparator**: the current guide accepts PNG/JPEG assets and ordered frame histories with `TypeSafe-Danger-Zone-Images: true`. The stale indexed text-only description is superseded by the live image guide. The checked model page maps stable/latest to 1.13 and preview to 1.14; pin the actual version when evaluating. Still frames are not a native video stream, and model confidence is not an independently established correctness score. A candidate-verification request must include an unknown/none option; reranking cannot recover a true class absent from its candidate set. No Jev ISL accuracy was established. ([Jev images](https://docs.typesafe.ai/images), [models](https://docs.typesafe.ai/models), [confidence](https://docs.typesafe.ai/confidence))

Laya supports bounded text/structured-state decisions. The supplied MLX port is Python/macOS and reports short-question timing on an M3 Max, not an iPhone. A separate `laya-ANE` repository supplies a real SwiftUI/Core ML iOS candidate, so the earlier blanket claim that no iPhone route exists is incorrect. Neither that source nor other Core ML conversions proves target-device parity, memory use or latency. Decision engines should earn their complexity by reducing wrong speech and repair burden against simple rules/templates, using only input Ishara actually possesses. Google SL2T's multilingual training announcement does not establish a public ISL developer API; initial product coverage and training-language breadth are different. ([Laya MLX](https://github.com/mizorewww/laya-mlx), [Laya ANE](https://github.com/vipuldivyanshu92/laya-ANE), [Core ML conversion](https://huggingface.co/FluidInference/laya-coreml), [Google announcement](https://deepmind.google/blog/putting-sign-language-ai-into-users-hands/))

## Bolna must fit the workflow rather than change it

Three authenticated read-only GET requests established working Bolna authentication and English/Hindi voice-catalog access. They did not create agents, synthesize audio, inspect phone inventories or place calls. A voice catalog is not a text-to-audio endpoint. The inspected public TTS, custom-provider, SDK, calling, event and index pages did **not establish standalone arbitrary-text synthesis hosted by Bolna**. In particular, Custom TTS's `/audio/speech` is an endpoint the developer supplies for Bolna to call; it is not a Bolna synthesis URL for Ishara. This bounded not-found result leaves room for vendor clarification without inventing an API. ([Voice catalog](https://www.bolna.ai/docs/api-reference/voice/overview), [Custom TTS](https://www.bolna.ai/docs/custom-tts.md), [documentation index](https://www.bolna.ai/docs/llms.txt))

The Web Call SDK creates a browser-to-agent WebRTC session, asks for microphone access, and documents no general send-text, transcript-event or raw-audio-output method. Adapting such a session into an output-only speech source beside a native call would require vendor support and specific audio-session, trigger, routing and lifecycle proof. It is an engineering hypothesis, not a ready native Phone integration. Custom LLM streaming and graph event injection apply inside agent sessions; they do not give Bolna access to the unrelated cellular call. Static-node audio is rendered when an agent is saved, so its cache timing cannot represent arbitrary newly signed sentences. ([SDK reference](https://www.bolna.ai/docs/developer-resources/sdks/web-call-api.md), [custom LLM](https://www.bolna.ai/docs/customizations/using-custom-llm.md), [event injection](https://www.bolna.ai/docs/graph-agent/event-injection.md), [static nodes](https://www.bolna.ai/docs/graph-agent/static-nodes.md))

**A verified Bolna test number is irrelevant to the required native-call path.** Trial destination verification belongs to Bolna-originated outbound calling, which is excluded. A future device test does need a consenting recipient to confirm remote audibility, but this is an ordinary carrier call from the user's phone, not Bolna verification or a second call. Local AAC speech should isolate platform feasibility first. Bolna qualification is a separate optional branch, and no billable sessions, outbound tests or product code are authorized by this research. ([Outbound restrictions](https://www.bolna.ai/docs/pricing/outbound-calling-concurrency.md), [Apple AAC sample](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/adding-synthesized-speech-to-calls))

The official buildathon page gives **31 October 2026** as the submission date and says ideas with a voice component qualify. The inspected page does not establish mandatory Bolna integration, judging weights, the exact deadline timezone or required submission assets. Confirm those organizer requirements before forcing a technically awkward integration for presumed eligibility. ([Official buildathon](https://www.bolna.ai/symphony/buildathon/))

## Device and data gates precede architecture commitments

The next evidence comes from the [validation plan](../../PLAN.md), not another broad vendor survey. Once implementation and an actual test call are separately authorized, inspect installed iOS and settings, verify a harmless fixed AAC utterance reaches the consenting recipient during the existing call, then add camera-only capture, then system captions, then sustained recognition. Record failure at each addition. Test mute, room noise, audio routes, interruptions, lock/app switching, caption readability, speech duplication and native hangup cleanup. The current public docs do not pass this combined-device gate.

In parallel, audit exact dialogue intents against existing licensed clips and compare isolated sequences with naturally signed phrase candidates. Use held-out signers where identity metadata permits; label approximate or unknown identity honestly. Include idle motion, unseen signs, partial framing, transitions, self-correction and context designed to tempt an incorrect model decision. Measure wrong automatically spoken utterances, repair burden and finish-to-remote-audio latency including commitment delay; author model-only Mac milliseconds are not a substitute. A pending buffer permits correction before playback, but speech already heard cannot be undone. Acceptance targets should be agreed before experiments; none is invented here.

### Unresolved founder questions

The first scope decision is the **readable return language and acceptable demonstration speech language**: is an English receptionist conversation with supported English captions acceptable, or is Hindi/mixed speech essential? The second is how much navigation and phone positioning is acceptable: does opening foreground Ishara after a normal call and propping the phone preserve enough of the everyday workflow? The third is what bounded conversation outcome matters: appointment inquiry, proposed-time exchange, or fully user-confirmed booking, including names and unforeseen questions? These choices determine whether the available system channel and phrase data cover the task.

The team also needs to decide whether cloud camera-frame disclosure for Jev is acceptable, whether a temporary explicit confirmation is preferable to mistaken automatic speech, and whether a separated-sign fallback is acceptable if natural phrase coverage fails. Those tradeoffs should remain visible rather than be hidden by a confidence threshold. A final organizer question is whether Bolna participation is mandatory and, if so, which non-telephony components count. The iPhone's exact OS/settings, distribution route and developer eligibility still need inspection. None of these questions is evidence that service-originated calls are back in scope.

## Conclusion

The project becomes more engineerable when the operating system retains ownership of calling and return captions, while Ishara focuses on faithful outgoing communication. That division removes the need to recreate telephony but restricts context-aware automation: the user, rather than the decision model, interprets the remote conversation. The next phase should convert demonstrated device behavior and audited phrase coverage into requirements; architecture and tickets remain unstarted until those decisions are made.

If either the required return language or concurrent capture/audio/caption composition fails, record the failed requirement and discuss scope explicitly. Do not silently replace an ordinary own-number call with a provider call or represent a staged recognition demo as natural ISL translation. The defensible hackathon result is a narrow capability with clear failure and repair behavior, backed by actual device evidence and candid limits on participant validation.
